The machine does not know what a word means. The machine knows which word came
next. That is the whole trick, and it is worth saying twice, because the trick is
smaller than it looks. The machine reads a great deal of text. The machine counts
which word followed which other word. Then the machine picks a word, looks at
what followed that word before, and picks one of those. It does this again. It
does this again, and again, until the sentence runs out of room.

A person writing a sentence knows where the sentence is going. The machine does
not know where the sentence is going. The machine knows only the last word or
two, and a table of what usually comes next. So the sentence wanders. Sometimes
the sentence wanders somewhere good, and a reader says that the machine is
clever. Sometimes the sentence wanders somewhere silly, and a reader says that
the machine is broken. Neither reader is right, because the machine was doing the
same small thing both times.

Read that again. The machine was doing the same small thing both times. The
clever sentence and the silly sentence came out of one table of counts. Nothing
in the table knows about towers, or dawn, or shapes on a shelf. The table knows
that the word the is often followed by the word machine, and that the word
machine is often followed by the word does, and that the word does is sometimes
followed by the word not.

You are going to build that table today. You are going to build it out of a
list, a loop, and a dictionary, which are three things you already have. Then you
are going to let it write sentences, and you are going to laugh at some of them,
and then you are going to know something about the programs that write text for
people. You will know that the program is counting. You will know what it is
counting. That is more than most people know.
